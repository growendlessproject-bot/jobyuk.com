Straight-Talking Guide Updated for 2026/27 UK Tax Year

Where does your paycheck actually go? (2026/27 UK Tax Explained Simply)

Job offers always quote your gross annual pay, but that is never what hits your bank account on the last Friday of the month. In the UK, your employer deducts income tax (PAYE) and National Insurance before you get paid. Here is exactly what HMRC takes and why:

0% Tax

Personal Allowance

£0 to £12,570

Your first £12,570 is completely tax-free cash. (Keep in mind: if you earn over £100,000, HMRC reduces this allowance by £1 for every £2 earned above £100k until it hits zero at £125,140).

20% Tax

Basic Rate Band

£12,571 to £50,270

You pay 20p on every pound earned in this range. If you earn £35,000, you don't pay 20% on the full 35k — only on the £22,430 above your personal allowance.

40% Tax

Higher Rate Band

£50,271 to £125,140

Every pound between £50,271 and £125,140 is taxed at 40p. Any pension contributions you make in this bracket also get 40% tax relief.

45% Tax

Additional Rate Band

Over £125,140

The top UK tax rate. Anything you earn over £125,140 is taxed at 45%, with zero personal allowance.

National Insurance: The 8% Rate Explained

Class 1 National Insurance funds the NHS, state pensions, and statutory sick pay. Here is the breakdown for employed workers:

  • 8% Main Rate: You pay 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270 a year (£1,048 to £4,189 a month).
  • 2% Upper Rate: On anything above £50,270 a year, National Insurance drops down to just 2%.

The Pension Trick: Why Salary Sacrifice is a No-Brainer

Most UK jobs automatically enrol you into a workplace pension (usually 5% from you, 3% from your boss). If your employer offers Salary Sacrifice, take it. Your pension contribution gets deducted before tax and National Insurance are calculated — which means you build your retirement fund faster while paying HMRC significantly less tax every single month.
